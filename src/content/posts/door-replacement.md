A replacement window in Lebanon County costs $500 to $2,200 installed depending on the frame material, and a whole house of eight to twelve windows lands between $8,000 and $18,000. An entry door runs $800 to $6,000 depending on what it is made of. Those ranges are wide because a vinyl double-hung and a wood-clad picture window are different products, so the first job is deciding what you actually need.

Typical regional ranges, not a quote. Windows are priced per opening after we measure.

## Signs it is time

- Condensation between the panes: the seal has failed and the insulating gas is gone.
- Sashes that stick, will not stay up, or let in a draft you can feel with a hand.
- Rot in a wood sill or frame, soft to a screwdriver.
- Single-pane glass with storm windows. The combination is worse than a modern double-pane unit.
- A door that has to be lifted to latch, daylight at the threshold, or a deadbolt that no longer lines up.

One failed window in a house of good ones is a repair. Several, or any rot, is a replacement conversation.

## Window replacement cost by frame

| Frame | Per window, installed | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Vinyl | $500 to $1,100 | The standard choice; no painting, good insulation, limited colors |
| Fiberglass | $900 to $1,700 | Stronger and more stable than vinyl, paintable, handles dark colors in sun |
| Composite | $1,000 to $1,800 | Wood-fiber and resin, between vinyl and wood in look and price |
| Wood or wood-clad | $1,200 to $2,200 and up | The look for old houses; aluminum or vinyl cladding outside cuts the upkeep |

Bay, bow and picture windows cost more than a standard double-hung. Black exterior frames add a little. For a whole house, eight to twelve windows commonly run $8,000 to $18,000.

For most Lebanon County houses we recommend fiberglass for the entry: it does not rust like steel, does not swell like wood, takes paint or a wood-grain stain, and the better ones carry long warranties.

## What is in the price besides the window

- **Measuring and ordering.** Every opening is measured; windows are made to size and take two to six weeks.
- **Removal.** Old sashes and frames out. In masonry houses, a pocket replacement keeps the brick opening; in frame houses, a full-frame replacement is sometimes needed when the old frame is rotten.
- **Installation.** Shimmed plumb and level, foamed, flashed, caulked.
- **Interior finish.** New stops, sometimes new casing and a stool.
- **Exterior trim.** Aluminum capping or new wood trim.
- **Disposal.**

A quote that is far below the others usually skips the flashing or the interior finish. Ask what is included.

## The energy ratings, in plain language

Every window carries a label with numbers. Two matter in Pennsylvania:

- **U-factor** is how much heat escapes. Lower is better. Look for 0.30 or under for our climate.
- **Solar heat gain coefficient (SHGC)** is how much sun heat comes in. Lower blocks summer heat; higher helps in winter. Around 0.30 is a sensible middle for Lebanon County.

Double-pane glass with a low-E coating and argon gas hits both numbers. Triple-pane costs more and helps most on a north wall or a very cold exposure. The [Department of Energy](https://www.energy.gov) explains the labels in more detail. New windows do cut drafts and bills, but the bigger comfort gain is usually in how the room feels near the glass in January.

## Windows in Lebanon County houses

- **Brick and stone houses** in Lebanon, Annville and the farm townships get pocket replacements in the masonry opening. Each opening is a slightly different size.
- **Frame houses** with rotten sills get full-frame replacements and sometimes new sheathing around the opening.
- **Rowhomes** have one or two walls that face weather; we focus the budget there. In a [South Philadelphia rowhome](/projects/south-philadelphia-windows-and-front-door/) every window went in during May so the house was sealed before drywall, and the front door and railings came last.
- **New rooms and additions** get new-construction windows with nailing fins, like the three black-frame windows in [this room](/projects/black-frame-window-installation/).

## Doors: what to decide

- **Material**, as above.
- **Glass.** A half-light or an oval panel brings light into a dark entry; a solid door is more private.
- **Swing and hardware.** Keep the swing if it works; change it only if the door hits something.
- **Sidelights and transoms** add light and cost; they also mean a wider opening.
- **The threshold and sweep.** The parts that stop the draft at the bottom. We replace both.

## Timeline

Order to installation is two to six weeks for standard windows. Installation itself is about an hour per window plus trim, so a whole house is one to three days. An entry door is half a day; a patio door, a day.

## The law and the warranty

Pennsylvania requires a written contract for any home improvement job over $500 and registration with the Attorney General for contractors doing more than $5,000 a year. Windows come with a manufacturer warranty on the glass seal and frame, often 20 years to lifetime; keep the labels and the paperwork, which is why we leave the stickers on until the final walkthrough.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does it cost to replace windows in Lebanon County?** $500 to $1,100 per vinyl window installed, $900 to $2,200 for fiberglass, composite or wood. Eight to twelve windows, $8,000 to $18,000.

**How much is a new front door?** $800 to $2,000 for steel, $1,500 to $3,500 for fiberglass, $2,500 and up for wood, installed.

**Should I replace all the windows at once?** It is cheaper per window and the house looks consistent. If the budget says no, do the weather side first.

**Vinyl or fiberglass?** Vinyl for most houses. Fiberglass for dark colors, big openings or a house you plan to keep for decades.
