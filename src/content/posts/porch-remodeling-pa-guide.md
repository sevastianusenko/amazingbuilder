A new deck in Lebanon County costs $20 to $36 per square foot in pressure-treated wood and $36 to $66 in composite, which puts a typical 12-by-16 deck between $5,500 and $13,000. Porches run higher because they have roofs: $30 to $60 per square foot open, $70 to $135 covered or screened. And the cheapest deck of all is the one you already have, resurfaced, if the frame is sound.

Typical regional ranges, not a quote. A deck is priced after we look at the ground and the house wall it attaches to.

## Deck cost

| Deck | Per square foot, installed | 12 by 16 deck |
|---|---|---|
| Pressure-treated wood | $20 to $36 | $4,000 to $7,000 |
| Composite | $36 to $66 | $7,000 to $13,000 |
| Resurfacing only, composite boards on a sound frame | Roughly half of new | Varies with railings and stairs |

Larger, multi-level, or decks on steep lots cost more. Railings, stairs and a permit are on top of the square-foot number: permits in Lebanon and Lancaster County municipalities run $100 to $500. Labor is 40 to 60 percent of the total.

## Porch cost

| Porch | Per square foot |
|---|---|
| Open porch, no roof | $30 to $60 |
| Covered porch | $70 to $135 |
| Screened porch | $70 to $130 |
| Wraparound or all-season | $50 to $150 |

A modest front porch starts around $4,000. A large wraparound or a finished all-season porch with heat, lighting and custom trim can run well past $50,000 to $90,000. Porches cost more than decks because of the roof: framing, roofing, tying into the house, and often electrical.

## Resurface or rebuild: the first question

The decking boards are the skin. The frame is the skeleton. If the skeleton is good, new boards on it cost about half of a new deck. We check four things before we decide, and we do it for free at the walkthrough:

1. **The ledger**, the board that attaches the deck to the house. It must be bolted and flashed. A nailed-only or rotten ledger is the number one cause of deck collapses and it means a rebuild.
2. **The joists**, probed with an awl, especially the tops where water sat under the old boards.
3. **The posts and footings.** Posts in soil rot from the bottom. Footings must be below frost line, 36 inches in Pennsylvania.
4. **The connections.** Hangers, post bases, bolts. Old decks are often missing some.

## Wood or composite

| | Pressure-treated wood | Composite |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Lower | About 60 to 80 percent more for the boards |
| Upkeep | Stain or seal every 2 to 3 years | Wash once a year |
| Look | Real wood, greys if not stained | Consistent, wood-grain finish, color lasts |
| Feel | Can splinter; cooler in sun | No splinters; hotter in full sun |
| Life | 10 to 15 years with care | 25 years and more; most carry 25-year warranties |
| Frame | Treated wood either way | Treated wood either way |

Our honest advice: if you will stain the deck every other year, wood is a fine value. If you know you will not, composite costs more once and nothing after. Most of our clients choose composite boards on a treated frame.

## Railings and stairs

Railings are a separate decision and a real cost. Treated wood railings are cheapest and need the same staining as the deck. Vinyl railings are white, maintenance-free and chunky. Aluminum and composite railings are slimmer and cost more. Black metal balusters in a wood or composite top rail is the look most people want now. Stairs are priced by the step; a wide stair, like the one on our [vinyl-railing deck](/projects/deck-with-vinyl-railings/), makes the yard part of the deck.

## Building it right in Pennsylvania

- **Footings below frost line.** Thirty-six inches here. Shallow footings heave and the deck racks.
- **Flashed ledger, bolted through.** The one detail that matters most.
- **Joists at 16 inches** for composite, which is heavier and spans less than wood.
- **Hidden fasteners** on composite so there are no screw heads to catch water or feet.
- **Gapping by the chart.** Composite moves with temperature; the gap depends on the temperature the day it goes down.
- **Picture-frame edges** so no cut ends show.
- **Permit and inspection.** Nearly every municipality in Lebanon and Lancaster County requires one for a deck attached to the house.

## Porches on Lebanon County houses

Front porches on the boroughs' older houses are usually wood on a masonry base, with columns and a roof that has been patched for a century. The common jobs are new floorboards and steps, new columns or posts, railings to current code height, and a ceiling. A porch rebuild is carpentry plus roofing, and we price the two separately so you can see what is structure and what is finish. Screened porches on newer houses in the townships are the other common request, usually added over an existing deck or patio.

## Timeline

A deck resurfacing is two to four days. A new 12-by-16 deck is one to two weeks including footings and inspection. A covered or screened porch is two to four weeks. Spring and fall book first; a deck built in late fall is ready for the first warm weekend.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does a deck cost in Lebanon County?** $20 to $36 per square foot in treated wood, $36 to $66 in composite. A 12-by-16 deck, $5,500 to $13,000 with railings and stairs.

**Can my old deck be resurfaced?** If the ledger, joists and posts pass inspection, yes, for roughly half the cost of new. We check for free.

**Do I need a permit?** For a deck attached to the house or above a certain height, yes, in almost every township. We pull it.

**Composite or wood?** Composite if you will not stain. Wood if you will and the budget is tight.
