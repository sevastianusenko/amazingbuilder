Almost no remodel returns more than it costs at resale. That is the honest starting point, and it is fine, because you also get to live in the house. The useful question is which projects return most of their cost while you own the house and make it sell faster and for more when you leave. In Pennsylvania the answer is consistent year to year: the exterior, the kitchen, the bathrooms, and anything that fixes a problem a buyer's inspector would find.

Typical regional ranges, not a quote.

## Returns by project

| Project | Typical cost in our area | Typical return at resale |
|---|---|---|
| Kitchen remodel, mid-range | $25,000 to $60,000 | 70 to 80 percent |
| Bathroom renovation | $12,000 to $30,000 | 65 to 75 percent |
| Basement finishing | $25,000 to $50,000 | 60 to 70 percent |
| Exterior: siding, windows, doors | $10,000 to $40,000 | About 60 percent |
| Energy upgrades: windows, insulation, air sealing | $5,000 to $15,000 | 50 to 80 percent, plus lower bills every year |

The pattern: moderate projects in a modest house return more than lavish ones. A $100,000 kitchen in a $300,000 house returns a fraction of its cost; a $35,000 kitchen in the same house returns most of it.

## What buyers in Lebanon and Lancaster County actually pay for

We see it from the contractor side, through what owners ask for before listing and what their agents tell them to fix:

- **A kitchen that does not need work.** Not a showpiece. Clean cabinets, a solid counter, decent appliances, good light. Buyers subtract the cost of a kitchen they will have to redo, and they subtract more than it would cost.
- **Bathrooms that match.** One new bathroom and one from 1974 reads as a project. Two modest updated bathrooms read as done.
- **A dry basement.** Finished or not, a basement with water marks on the walls scares every buyer. A dry, clean, finished basement adds usable square footage at the lowest cost in the house.
- **The front of the house.** The door, the windows, the railings, the paint. Buyers decide a lot before they get out of the car.
- **Nothing on the inspection report.** Old wiring, a cracked stair tread, a rotten sill, a missing handrail. Each item is a negotiation and a reason to walk.

## The exterior: first and most reliable

Siding, a new entry door, replacement windows and a repaired porch are the projects with the steadiest returns, because they are the ones every buyer sees and the ones that signal the house has been cared for. A new front door is one of the few projects that routinely returns close to its full cost. Our [windows and doors guide](/blog/door-replacement/) and [siding guide](/blog/siding-replacement-cost-lebanon-county-pa/) have the ranges.

In a [South Philadelphia rowhome](/projects/south-philadelphia-windows-and-front-door/) the new windows, door and iron railings were a small share of the budget and changed the whole street view of the house.

## Kitchens and bathrooms: the rooms that sell a house

A mid-range kitchen, new cabinets and counters on the same layout, is the single most effective project for resale in our area, because buyers compare kitchens before anything else. The rooms do not have to be big. The [small-kitchen guide](/blog/small-kitchen-remodel-ideas-lebanon-county-pa/) covers how a borough kitchen gets the same effect for less.

Bathrooms return well when the house is short of them. Adding a second bathroom, or a bathroom to a finished basement, changes which buyers will look at the house at all. A [walk-in shower](/blog/walk-in-shower-conversion-cost-pa/) appeals to older buyers; a tub somewhere in the house keeps families interested.

## Basements: the cheapest square footage

A finished basement adds a room for less than half the cost of an addition because the foundation and roof already exist. It returns 60 to 70 percent at resale and more than that if the alternative was a buyer seeing a damp, dark space. The condition is the one in our [basement guide](/blog/basement-finishing-pa/): the slab and walls have to be dry first.

## Energy upgrades: value you collect twice

Windows, insulation, air sealing and a modern heating system return part of their cost at sale and all of their savings while you live there. In a drafty Lebanon County farmhouse the heating bill is a real number on a buyer's mind. Keep the paperwork; appraisers and buyers give credit for documented work.

## Projects that rarely pay back

- **Swimming pools.** Costly, seasonal, and a liability to some buyers.
- **Luxury finishes in a modest neighborhood.** A $20,000 range in a $250,000 house.
- **Removing a bedroom** to enlarge another. Bedroom count drives value in most of our market.
- **Converting the garage** to living space. Buyers want the garage.
- **Highly personal choices.** A dark red kitchen, a themed bathroom, built-ins designed around one hobby.
- **Over-improving a rental.** A rental needs durable, not custom.

None of these are wrong if you want them. They just do not come back at sale.

## Curb appeal on a small budget

The cheapest value projects are outside: paint the front door, replace the house numbers and the light, fix the railing, pressure-wash the walk, re-mulch, and repair whatever is loose on the porch. A few hundred to a few thousand dollars, and it changes the photo that goes on the listing.

## Timing

If you are selling within a year, do the exterior, the paint and the repairs, and only the kitchen or bath if they are clearly dated. If you are staying five years or more, do the projects you want in the order our [budget guide](/blog/remodeling-cost-pa-budget/) suggests: water and structure first, then systems, then surfaces. You will get the use and most of the value.

Every project over $500 needs a written contract from a contractor registered with the [Pennsylvania Attorney General](https://www.attorneygeneral.gov/). Permitted, inspected work is also work you can prove at sale; an unpermitted basement cannot be counted as living space by an appraiser.

## Frequently asked questions

**What remodel adds the most value in Pennsylvania?** A mid-range kitchen in a house that needs one, and the exterior: door, windows, siding. Both return 70 percent or more of their cost in a typical year.

**Does a finished basement add value?** Yes, 60 to 70 percent of its cost, and it widens the pool of buyers, as long as it is dry and permitted.

**Is it worth remodeling before selling?** Repairs and the exterior, almost always. A full kitchen, only if the current one would scare buyers off; otherwise price the house to reflect it.

**Do permits matter for value?** Yes. Unpermitted work cannot be counted by an appraiser and shows up as a problem at inspection.
