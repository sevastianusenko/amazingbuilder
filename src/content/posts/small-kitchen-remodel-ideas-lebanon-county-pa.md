Half the kitchens in Lebanon County are small. The borough rowhomes in Lebanon and Annville, the post-war ranches in Myerstown and Palmyra, and the old farmhouse kitchens that were built for a wood stove all have one thing in common: a room under 120 square feet with a window, a door and three walls that do not agree with each other. The good news is that a small kitchen is the cheapest kitchen to make excellent, because every upgrade is a few feet of material.

Here is what actually works, from the jobs we do, with honest cost ranges at the end.

## Start with the layout you have

Moving plumbing is the most expensive thing in any kitchen, and in a small one it rarely buys enough. The three layouts we see:

- **Galley.** Two parallel runs with a walkway between. Keep the sink and range on the same wall if they are already there, put tall storage at the ends, and keep the walkway at least 42 inches.
- **L-shape.** Two walls, one corner. The corner is where storage goes to die; a lazy susan or a blind-corner pullout fixes it.
- **Single wall.** One run, usually in a rowhome. Everything depends on height, so this is the layout where uppers to the ceiling matter most.

## Storage: go up, then go deep

**Uppers to the ceiling.** Standard uppers stop 18 inches short of an 8-foot ceiling and leave a dusty shelf. A 42-inch upper, or a 30-inch upper with a 12-inch stacked cabinet above, adds a third more storage for the same floor space. We did exactly this in a [South Philadelphia rowhome kitchen](/projects/south-philadelphia-rowhome-kitchen/).

**Tall pantry cabinets.** One 24-inch-wide pantry beside the refrigerator holds what four base cabinets hold. If the room has a dead end, that is where it goes.

**Drawers instead of doors in the base cabinets.** A drawer brings the back of the cabinet to you. Three drawers in a 24-inch base replace a door and a shelf you have to kneel to reach.

**Pullouts in the narrow spots.** A 9-inch pullout beside the range holds spices and oils. A 6-inch one holds trays.

**Open shelves only where you will keep them neat.** They look good in photos. Over a sink or beside a window they are useful; over the range they collect grease.

## Light and color that make the room bigger

- **One color on cabinets and backsplash.** White shaker with white subway tile reads as one surface and the eye does not stop at the corners.
- **Light counters.** A pale quartz bounces light. A black counter in a small kitchen absorbs it.
- **Under-cabinet lights.** The single cheapest upgrade with the biggest effect. The counters are where you work, and the ceiling light is behind you.
- **Recessed cans instead of one center fixture.** Four small cans light the corners the center fixture never reached.
- **A bigger window, if there is any budget for it.** Replacing a small window over the sink with a wider one is exterior work, but in a kitchen that gets daylight from one side it changes everything.

## Appliances sized for the room

- **24-inch dishwasher** is standard; an 18-inch one fits where a standard will not and holds a day of dishes for two people.
- **Counter-depth refrigerator.** Six inches shallower than a standard, which in a galley is the difference between a walkway and a squeeze. Look for [ENERGY STAR models](https://www.energystar.gov/products/refrigerators).
- **30-inch range** with an over-the-range microwave, or a 24-inch range in a rowhome kitchen. We tell owners up front whether the microwave hood can vent outside; on a party wall it usually cannot.
- **Skip the double oven and the separate cooktop.** Each one costs cabinet space a small kitchen does not have.

## Ideas that do not work in a small kitchen

- **An island.** Unless the room is at least 12 feet wide, an island makes a walkway, not a kitchen. A narrow peninsula off the end of a run is the small-kitchen version.
- **Dark cabinets on all walls.** They can work with a lot of light. In a north-facing rowhome kitchen they make a cave.
- **A farmhouse sink in a 24-inch base.** The sink needs a 33-inch or 36-inch base. Plan for it or skip it.
- **Removing the only wall of uppers for open shelves.** You will put everything on the counter.

## When to add space instead

A kitchen bump-out or addition runs $200 to $400 per square foot in our area, because every added foot needs foundation, roof, cabinets, plumbing, electrical and a permit. Even a small addition carries the full cost of structural work. For most houses, remodeling inside the existing footprint is the better use of money. The exception is a farmhouse with a pantry or a porch behind the kitchen that can be absorbed without new foundation; we have opened those up and it is worth looking at.

## Hiring for a small kitchen

A small kitchen leaves less room for error. A contractor who mostly builds big open kitchens may not have the instincts for a tight galley. Ask to see small kitchens they have done. Pennsylvania's Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act requires a written contract for any job over $500 and registration with the Attorney General for contractors doing more than $5,000 a year; ask for the number. [NARI](https://www.nari.org) and [This Old House](https://www.thisoldhouse.com/kitchens) are good places to read before you call anyone.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does a small kitchen remodel cost in Lebanon County?** A refresh, $3,000 to $10,000. A mid-range remodel, $10,500 to $20,000. A full remodel with new cabinets, $20,000 to $35,000.

**Should I take the cabinets to the ceiling?** Yes, in almost every small kitchen. The top shelf holds what you use twice a year, and the room looks taller.

**Can a small kitchen have an island?** Only if there is 42 inches of walkway on every side after the island goes in. Measure before you fall in love with one.

**Is it worth adding a bump-out?** Rarely, at $200 to $400 per square foot. Fix the layout and the storage first.
