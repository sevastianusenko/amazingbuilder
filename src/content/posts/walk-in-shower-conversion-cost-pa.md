Converting a tub to a walk-in shower is the single most requested bathroom job in Lebanon County, and the range is wide: $6,000 for a basic conversion that keeps the drain where it is, $12,000 to $20,000 for a curbless shower built for aging in place, and $18,000 to $30,000 or more when the tile, glass and fixtures are top of the line. Most owners land between $6,000 and $20,000.

These are typical regional ranges, not a quote. The number for your bathroom depends on three questions below, and we answer them at the walkthrough.

## Cost by tier

| Tier | Typical cost | What it includes |
|---|---|---|
| Basic conversion | $6,000 to $12,000 | Same footprint, same drain location, tiled or acrylic surround, framed or sliding glass door |
| Curbless, zero threshold | $12,000 to $20,000 | Floor lowered or reframed, linear drain, wider entry, blocking for grab bars, bench |
| Luxury walk-in | $18,000 to $30,000 and up | Large-format or stone tile, rain head plus handheld or body sprays, frameless glass, built-in bench and niche |

## The three questions that set the price

**1. Does the drain stay where it is?** A tub drain is at one end of the tub. A shower drain is usually in the middle of the floor or along one wall. Moving it means opening the floor and sometimes the ceiling below. If the new shower can use the old drain location, you stay in the basic tier.

**2. Curb or no curb?** A standard shower has a tiled curb, a few inches high, that holds the water in. A curbless shower has no step: the floor itself slopes to a linear drain. To get that slope the subfloor has to be lowered or the joists notched and reframed, which is carpentry on top of tile. It is the right answer for anyone planning to stay in the house into their seventies, and the wrong place to economize.

**3. What is the glass?** Glass is the part people forget to budget:

| Enclosure | Typical cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Shower curtain | $50 to $300 | Cheapest, needs washing, the least finished look |
| Framed glass door | $400 to $1,200 | Metal frame around the panels, budget friendly |
| Semi-frameless | $800 to $2,000 | One or two slim posts, cleaner look |
| Frameless | $1,200 to $3,000 and up | Heavy glass, the easiest to keep clean, the premium look |

Glass is measured after the tile is finished and takes two to three weeks to arrive. We order it the day the tile is done.

## What the job involves

1. **Demolition.** The tub, surround and often the wall tile come out. In older Lebanon County houses the walls behind are plaster and come off to the studs.
2. **Plumbing.** A licensed plumber sets the new valve at shower height, adds a second outlet if there will be a handheld, and sets or moves the drain.
3. **Framing.** Blocking in the walls for grab bars, even if you do not want them today. A niche framed between studs. For curbless, the floor work.
4. **Waterproofing.** Cement board on the walls, a membrane over it and over the pan, flood-tested before tile. This is the step that decides whether the shower is still dry in fifteen years.
5. **Tile.** Walls, floor, niche, curb or linear drain.
6. **Glass, fixtures, fan, paint.**

A basic conversion takes about a week of work; curbless and luxury two to three weeks, plus the wait for glass.

## Who converts, and why

- **Owners staying put.** Stepping over a tub wall is the most common fall risk in a house. A curbless shower with a bench and grab bars removes it. Most of our curbless jobs are for owners in their sixties planning ahead.
- **Families with two bathrooms.** Keep the tub in one, convert the other. That is what we did in a [South Philadelphia rowhome](/projects/south-philadelphia-rowhome-bathrooms/): a tub bath upstairs and a compact shower room with a curved glass enclosure.
- **Small bathrooms.** A corner shower with a curved or neo-angle enclosure frees floor space a tub takes.

Who should not convert: a house with only one bathroom and small children, or an owner planning to sell to a family within a few years. Buyers with kids want a tub somewhere in the house.

## Things to check before you convert

- **Water pressure and the water heater.** A rain head and body sprays want more water than a tub spout. A small electric water heater may not keep up.
- **The floor.** A tiled shower floor is heavy and has to be flat. Bouncy floors in old houses need reinforcing first.
- **Ventilation.** A shower throws more steam at the walls than a bath. The fan has to be ducted outside, not into the attic.
- **The drain size.** Linear drains and rain heads move a lot of water; a two-inch drain line is standard for a shower where a tub often had an inch and a half.
- **Permits.** Plumbing changes need a permit in every Lebanon County municipality we work in. We pull it.

## Materials that make sense here

Large-format porcelain on the walls with a small mosaic or textured porcelain on the floor for grip is the combination we recommend most. It looks like stone, costs less, and does not need sealing. Natural stone is beautiful and it is a maintenance commitment in a wet room; we install it when an owner wants it and knows that.

## What the law says

Pennsylvania's Home Improvement Consumer Protection Act requires contractors doing more than $5,000 of work a year to register with the [Attorney General](https://www.attorneygeneral.gov/businesses-and-organizations/home-improvement-contractor-registration/), a written contract for any job over $500, and a three-business-day cancellation right. In houses built before 1978, demolition that disturbs old paint falls under the [EPA lead-safe rules](https://www.epa.gov/lead). Both apply to a shower conversion.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does a tub-to-shower conversion cost in Lebanon County?** $6,000 to $12,000 when the drain stays put and the tile is standard. $12,000 to $20,000 for a curbless shower. $18,000 and up with premium tile, frameless glass and multiple heads.

**Can I get a curbless shower in an old house?** Usually, but the floor has to be lowered or reframed, which is why it costs more. We check the joists first.

**How long is the bathroom out of service?** About a week for a basic conversion, two to three for curbless or luxury, plus the glass wait, during which the shower can be used with a curtain.

**Acrylic or tile?** An acrylic surround is quicker and cheaper and fine for a basic conversion. Tile lasts longer, looks better and is the only choice for curbless.
